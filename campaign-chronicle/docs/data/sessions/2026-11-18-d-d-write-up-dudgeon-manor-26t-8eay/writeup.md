As the dust settles on their previous adventure, rescuing Pip and recovering Tolbein, the adventurers return to Falcon’s Lodge and enjoy some well-earned rest.

The party sinks into the welcome bosom of a comfortable routine. Days pass.
Tolbin takes his place at the bar but, being just a head, he requires Brutus’ help to serve his pints. The days become weeks.

Dander uses the time to study, Magnus works on himself with training, Kuro has a new philosophy student in Farq and Vitrum agrees to construct a grand chandelier for Imrik. He spends his days working on the commission and his evenings behind closed doors, trying to discover how he ended up here by unsuccessfully attempting to contact his patron.

This humdrum existence is interrupted by the initially inconspicuous arrival of Tamsin, an exhausted dragonborn postal worker looking for a bit of R&R while on a package delivery (**and suddenly the routine’s bosom won't be the only bosom sunk into today...**)

After initially viewing Tamsin as a slave labourer, Imrik seizes his moment. The pair quickly retire to his bedroom, to show her his “Pigley von Pigglestein” and complete some package deliveries. Sparks fly, and what sounds like Princess Leia squawking echoes throughout the lodge.

During this dalliance, Farq spies a delivery note that fell out of Tamsin’s pocket in the rush up the stairs and derobing. The package she's delivering is addressed to someone called Mira. It contains medical supplies and comes with strict instructions to hand it off to one Pell Fenwick at the hospice on ice spire peak. The account holder however catches Farq's attention: Kadrack!! After consulting Kuro and misinterpreting his “tit for hat” advice, Farq pinches Tamsin’s clothing. Kuro sighs, noting her ID would have been more useful than an ill fitting hat…
Luckily Tamsin finds herself now _unable to walk_ and requests our heroes help to deliver a parcel, offering her ID and delivery instructions. The group knows what needs to be done.


After several days’ worth of travel up into the snow-capped mountains, the group arrive at the delivery address, an exclusive hospice for the elite in a cliff-side location that Ernst Blofield could only aspire to. Tired of debating the correct choice of approach to this almost-certainly dangerous location in the bitterly cold wind, the group makes the obvious choice, and walks directly in the front door.

Armed with Magnus transformed to resemble Tamsin and a less than likely story of many postal workers in training, the group proceeds 

They are greeted at the front desk by Orin, an attentive Swiss man dripping with charm and professionalism, where he offers the group tea. Magus attempts to answer Orin’s suspicion laced questions but finds himself losing control of his wild magic. Thankfully the effect is seemingly unnoticeable but the break in concentration sees Magnus return to his human form. 

Dander attempts to distract Orin from the sudden lack of dragonborn with a performance of a lifetime. Blaming the tea she howls until Orin fetches Voss, a portly and somewhat grumpy nurse, to handle Dander.  
Dander is led into a lounge, and seated next to the dotty elderly Dorothy - aka, the sweetest human alive. Dorothy’s train-of-thought ping pongs around but is mainly concerned - in ascending order - with her sweeties; the noises upstairs & downstairs; and the red squirrels (or the lack thereof). Terry takes a look around, there's something not quite right about the picture on the wall

Unsure of what to do, and getting hungry for dinner, the rest of the group all shuffle in behind Kuro, piggy-backing on this provided stealth boon, and they gradually begin sneaking through the staff-only corridors of the hospice, luckily undiscovered.

Farq obliviously ignores this stealth-based approach and marches through the hospice. He's taking his job as trainee postal work very seriously and is looking for Pell as instructed. He finds his way into the drug dispensary, where he meets Pell the pill pot putter, surrounded by all the various tinctures, potions and ointments that cater to the hospice patients' needs.
Pell, a handsome youthful woman, who seems a little bored by her tedious job of perpetually putting pills into the pill pots again and again, soon persuades Farq to pop a pair of particularly powerful pills. (Although this only takes a puny portion of persuasion on Farq’s part).
Plainly, this was a previously unexplored yet perpetually pursued pleasure for her, given her passionate penchant for psychedelic partying in the past.
Being the experienced raver and a terrible trip-buddy, Pell holds her own and rolls a high CON saving throw, beginning an eternal dance.
Meanwhile, Farq’s mental space increases exponentially, as he travels across space-time dimensions and experiences complete and utter mental dissolution of his ego. He finds himself at the feet of the Great One Eye Gruumsh and is berated by his God

The stealth party slips unnoticed into a sophisticated office, where on the grand wooden desk, they discover a gift wrapped box, surrounded by reams of paperwork and observation notes on Mira. The papers show the consignment the group delivered contains 3 doses of medicine for private use. Three doses of life-saving medicine for Kadrack’s daughter. They open the gift box on the table, it contains impeccable glass bird ornaments in various hues - one of which, Vitrum pockets.

Loud, commanding footsteps are heard through the Hospice’s corridors. Kadrack has arrived. Thankfully Dander can act as the group’s eyes and ears from the lounge, sucking on Dorothy’s sweeties, through the dominoes hat. Amazingly, no-one is ever found while the staff make their way around the hospice on their scheduled rounds.

The group then explores further, being rejoined by a sobering-up Farq, and enters the private room of the sickly bed-bound girl, Mira. Her father’s evil schemes have been part of him toiling away for years to cure her from her terminal illness. Naive from being cloistered from the outside world, Mira has an adventurous spirit and lusts to hear stories of past escapades, and to see the outside world. She is surrounded by her adored glass birds, and the group gifts her the one Vitrum stole from the office.

Desperately asking the group to help her discover what is downstairs, Mira provides them with a wax mould of the basement key that she had surreptitiously taken behind the backs of both her father and the hospice staff. 

The group now needs to manufacture an object from a negative mould. Conveniently there’s a new glassworker in their midst. Outside the hospice, bracing in the freezing blizzard wind, they turf out the contents of a stone planter; Imrik digs through his pockets and discovers (for some reason) he’s been carrying around a substantial amount of broken glass. Making sure that he crosses that off from his inventory, he places it in the planter and Magnus provides the immense heat required. Vitrum dons his double loupe, and using his glassblower kit’s blowpipe, tweezers and marvel, creates a glass key - quenching it in the snow like a Prince Rupert’s drop.

Sneaking around the side of the chateau, aided by the invocation of a silent image matte painting against the windows, the group make it to the entrance of the central care ward, where Dander is happily tucked up with Dorothy.

Having discovered someone tampering with the parcel in his office, Kadrack alerts Voss and is told to suspect anyone out of the ordinary. Such as a strange new group of posties…

Dander panics and goes invisible as Voss charges towards her. Dorothy, the real hero of the story - unexpectedly proves herself to be quite the formidable combatant - dropping to the floor and bowling through Voss. Dander attempts a humane sleep spell but Farq runs in and instantly KO’s Voss with a bonk on the noggin. The old nurse was no match for the barbarian.

The group looks around the room for the entrance to the basement. Seeing Terry scratching at the wall, Imrik excitedly removes the painting and reveals the stairway door. The unbelievably fragile glass key slides into the lock. A tense moment of carefully applied pressure… click!

Unlocking the door and descending down into the dank, dark basement, the group takes care not to trip on the cobbled steps, as their breaths condense on the chilled creepy air. The walls of the basement are lined with tubes pulsating with a very familiar looking ooze… The fluid is being pumped from the roof - where it is seemingly collected - down to the basement, before it heads back up to the hospice floors.

Tracing the simultaneous coming and going pipes down into the belly of the basement, they discover that through his macabre, unholy machinery, the fluid is being pumped through a number of lifeless-looking bodies adorning the wall! They are indeed alive, but only just.

Vitrum casts a piercing light using his orb, revealing to the group an infernal contract. A contract of preservation detailing the agreement between Kadrack and demons Belial and Ferni. The contract keeping Mira alive and damning those who enjoy a tasty dominoes.
There’s a giant lever. It should shut off this damned apparatus. But what will be of Mira?

Just as the group considers this, a dark imposing silhouette appears at the top of the stairs…
